## Owner direction already established

- Long trips are a major use case. Balance comfort, performance and style with less racer character.
- Concept A from round 02 is the preferred starting shape, derived from original concept 2. Previous concepts 4 and 5 informed the exploration.
- Compact controllable proportions with substantial highway presence; avoid visual bulk and a front-heavy appearance.
- Simpler visual surfaces and a clean electric identity. Preserve honest battery volume and cooling needs.
- Rider should not need to prop their torso on handlebars. Hands rest naturally on grips; relaxed shoulders, bent elbows and neutral wrists are the intended fit. Bar reach matters alongside height, seat and pegs.
- Small precise BAVUSO wordmark, understated technical luxury. Exact final lettering remains open. Technology should provide demonstrable product value; unvalidated performance is not a marketing claim.
- Matte black must be offered. White and sand are finish candidates, with launch color count still undecided. Prior grey remains historical reference, not a confirmed launch finish.
- Optional saddlebags should be possible as extras. Base bike is shown without bags; luggage mounts, load ratings and accessories still require engineering.

## Proposed visual direction for approval

One common A-derived body, medium clear windscreen, raised swept-back handlebars, flatter supportive padded rider saddle, integrated rear support, matte upper body with dark functional components, and one small titanium/tonal BAVUSO wordmark per side. Matte black is the anchor finish; white and sand show possible alternatives. The colors are visual references, not paint codes, gloss-unit specifications or approved physical samples.

The review asks the owner to approve overall appearance, finish direction, branding restraint and the intended comfort posture. Passenger accommodation, dimensions, structural geometry and launch color count are not implicitly approved by a visual selection. A depicted pillion area is not a passenger rating.

## Engineering translation and open work

Use engineering-handoff.md and designer-brief.md as the engineering baseline. The images must be reconciled with a real package rather than used as scaled drawings.

1. Build an adjustable seating buck and measured rider layout. Check bar sweep/reach, lever and wrist alignment, seat slope/support, knee and hip angles, ground reach, peg location, steering lock and cable/hose routing with different rider proportions and protective gear. The posture illustration uses no validated anthropometric model; do not derive exact dimensions from it. Normal operation can still involve temporary hand loads from braking, acceleration and bumps; the goal concerns ordinary supported riding posture.
2. Develop the ordinary upright rider and body together for the target total bike+rider CdA ≤0.33 m². Raised bars/upright posture may increase drag; no aerodynamic credit is earned by the render. Evaluate screen wake, helmet buffeting, cooling drag and 77 mph crosswind forces/moments, then correlate physical testing.
3. Retain the approximately 600 L × 260 W × 560 H mm battery-region reservation pending a validated module/cooling/service layout. Resolve pack removal, structural paths, fault venting away from rider, protection and isolation. Do not shrink the package to match an image.
4. Reconcile motor, inverter, onboard charger, charging controls, radiators/fans/pumps, wiring and coolant with actual installation drawings and service clearances. Shown apertures indicate cooling intent, not sized ducts. Stationary/hot-weather charging remains a separate thermal requirement. The current EMRAX v1.7 revision flag is documented in research/design-strategy.md; confirm supply and performance before fixing drive hardware.
5. Check 17-inch tire/wheel envelopes, full bump/steering/lean clearance, brakes, belt alignment/tension and shaft support. AI-generated drive, brake and suspension details are illustrative and can be incorrect. The belt-drive architecture is the intended study baseline.
6. Integrate native NACS inlet placement and real cable handling, stand stability, rain drainage, lock and service access. Port position is not selected in these side renders. Tesla network access remains unconfirmed; candidate AC hardware does not establish 120 V support.
7. Engineer discreet removable bag attachments and load paths without a permanent bulky rack; assess loaded mass/CG, handling, clearance, aero and heat. Passenger/payload and bag capacity are still owner/product decisions.
8. Reconcile mass with a complete bill of materials. Existing allocation is approximately 433 lb before contingency / 446 lb with it; preferred under-400 lb goal is not relaxed by visual approval. Body, seat, controls, lights and wiring share the existing 11 kg allocation.
9. Check applicable market requirements for lighting, mirrors, controls, braking and road equipment before placement is fixed. Select manufacturable panels, coatings, badge attachment and service strategy. Validate matte finish durability, cleaning, UV/weathering, abrasion and repair on physical samples.
