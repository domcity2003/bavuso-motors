# Bavuso Motors — product, brand and design research

October 25, 2026, US Central. Working research and design recommendations, not adopted specifications. Prepared with separate research agents for market positioning, rider experience, and product identity; mechanical synthesis by the primary agent.

## Starting point

The motorcycle is Bavuso's only product. Its proportions, riding experience, interface, charging behavior, finish, repairability and ownership support must communicate the same promise. A logo cannot compensate for contradictions between these things.

Recommended working promise: **a desirable electric road motorcycle that makes longer rides feel natural and manageable.** This is a product-development direction, not a claim that the current concept has achieved its range, charging or mass goals. The primary customer hypothesis is an experienced road rider who wants electric simplicity without giving up highway usefulness. Secondary hypotheses include returning riders and design-conscious EV owners; customer interviews and price testing are still required.

Research distinguishes owner goals, engineering proposals, published supplier references, calculations and design judgments. Manufacturer pages document advertised products and positioning; they do not prove customer demand or independently verify performance. No supplier outreach, purchasing or physical testing has occurred.

## Mechanical constraints translated into design work

The engineering handoff remains the concept baseline. These are constraints to preserve or investigate, not a released package.

| Area | Current basis | Consequence for design |
|---|---|---|
| Owner performance goals | 200 miles at 70 mph; 125+ mph; 77 mph comfort/stability study speed | Design normal riding posture and bodywork together. Do not substitute city range or a racing tuck. Top-speed duration is undecided. |
| Battery | 30.056 kWh study, 100s8p, approximately 92–97 kg depending on unproven integration | Central mass dominates the silhouette and chassis. Preserve compression, growth, protection, cooling, isolation and removal space. |
| Battery reservation | 600 fore/aft × 260 wide × 560 high mm; 87.36 L bounding reservation | Not a final rectangular battery. Shape only with a credible internal layout; do not shrink to the 34.93 L raw-cell total. |
| Rider/package relationship | Narrow knee region; adjustable contacts; initial solo packaging | Show rider in side/front/top views, boots and gloves included. Passenger/luggage requirements can change seat, subframe, loading and wake. |
| Drive | Frame-mounted single motor near swingarm pivot; fixed reduction; belt preferred | Allow shaft support, pulley diameters, belt alignment/tension through suspension movement, guards and wheel service. Motor body dimensions alone are insufficient. |
| Reference components | Motor Ø208 × 85 mm; inverter 330 × 188 × 97; AC charger 373 × 224 × 76.5 | Add mounts, connectors, hose/cable bend radii and tool access. Avoid impossible overlapping boxes or a decorative empty frame triangle. |
| Chassis | Fabricated metal chassis, double-sided swingarm, conventional fork/shock | Primary sketches should be credible with these choices. Pack cells are not chassis load paths. Structural enclosure contribution needs analysis. |
| Geometry exploration | Wheelbase 1,400–1,450 mm; seat 780–820; rake 24–26°; trail 95–110 mm | Study ranges, not fixed hard points. Seat width and sag affect reach; geometry must work with actual tires and mass distribution. |
| Wheels/clearance | 17-inch; 120/70 front, 160/60 rear; 120–140 mm wheel travel; ~150 mm static clearance study | Draw full steering, bump, tire growth, lean, brake and fender clearances. Static beauty-view clearance is insufficient. |
| Aero | Target total bike+rider CdA ≤0.33 m² in normal highway posture, cooling included | Front wheel/fork, shoulders, legs, screen, mirrors and rear wake all matter. A smooth-looking shell earns no numerical drag credit. |
| Wind stability | Evaluate 77 mph and yaw/gust response; proposed yaw ±5°, ±10°, ±15° | Side force and steering/roll/yaw moments must be evaluated. Large continuous side panels require particular scrutiny; no direction is proven stable. |
| Thermal | Separate battery/drive temperature control; stationary fan cooling; hot-air routing | Reserve intake, radiator/fan, duct and exit volumes. Keep heat off legs and fault venting separate from normal cooling and rider zones. |
| Hot-weather charging | Radiator cannot cool below ambient; refrigeration absent from existing allowance | Avoid a fixed fast-charge promise. A chiller could change mass, volume, cost and acoustic character. |
| Charging | Native NACS study, separate AC/DC paths, proposed 6.6 kW AC / up to 50 kW DC | Mock up cable reach, lean on stand, connector loading, gloves, lid clearance, drainage and status visibility. Confirm access separately from connector fit. |
| Home charging | Candidate AC unit lists 176–264 V input | Ordinary 120 V support remains a sourcing decision, not an existing feature. This materially affects apartment/garage ownership positioning. |
| Braking | Independent hydraulic brakes, ABS; initial dual 300–320 mm front / 220–240 rear reservations | Keep brake package, hoses and sensors real. Regen cannot replace friction-brake capability; brake feel must remain predictable when regen is limited. |
| Complete mass | 433 lb allocated / 446 with allowance; lightweight case 396 / 407 unproven | Under 400 lb remains owner preference. The 11 kg body/seat/controls/lights/wiring line is shared, not an 11 kg fairing budget. |
| Road equipment | Intended-market certification work not completed | Include real lamps, indicators, reflectors, mirrors, plate, horn and controls from the first proportion studies. Freeze placement only after applicable requirements are checked. |

### New supplier revision flag

The handoff cites EMRAX 208 v1.5. The manufacturer's current v1.7 detailed table lists **47 kW continuous for liquid cooling**, **75 Nm continuous torque**, and an **86 kW peak rating for 30 seconds**. It retains the 208 × 85 mm body and 10.3 kg liquid-cooled reference. It also announces a transition to next-generation motors. Its overview headline spans configurations; do not apply the 52 kW headline to the liquid-cooled variant. Airflow remains required even with liquid cooling. Recheck winding, loaded voltage, thermal margin, installation and supply before fixing the drive package; this memo does not silently replace the existing calculation baseline. [EMRAX 208 v1.7](https://emrax.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/EMRAX_208_datasheet_v1.7.pdf).

Tesla's current support information distinguishes site/access categories and manufacturer access; it does not establish Bavuso authorization. Cable reach and port placement also affect real station use. Design the parked charging experience, but keep network compatibility a validation gate. [Tesla support](https://www.tesla.com/support/charging/supercharging-other-evs).

## Full design scope

1. **Customer and commercial position:** primary use, current bike/alternative, willingness to pay, home-charging access, launch geography, sales/service model, production volume, warranty and residual-value confidence. Price is a design input because it changes tooling, materials and support economics. No defensible retail price is established yet.
2. **Proportions and identity:** recognizable silhouette, front/rear recognition, light signature, surface character, visible structure, graphic hierarchy, colors/materials/finishes, and how the bike looks with its actual rider and road equipment. Make the substantial battery look intentional.
3. **Rider fit and control:** inseam and reach combinations, seat width/pressure, knees/hips/wrists, adjustable bars/levers/pegs, sightlines, wind noise, buffeting, vibration, seat heat, mounting, low-speed balance and pushing/backing on slopes. Height alone is a poor fit specification.
4. **Everyday use:** wet/gloved controls, sun/night display legibility, cruise, predictable throttle/regen, remaining range with uncertainty, charging limits/errors, parking security, luggage and passenger decisions, rain spray and storage for small essentials.
5. **Ownership trust:** parts availability, diagnostic access, physical controls for essentials, update recovery, privacy, security, transport/towing, battery health information, repair manuals and a realistic warranty/service promise. A basic ride or charge should not depend on a working phone connection.
6. **Manufacture and repair:** assembly order, supplier availability, low-volume tooling, panel count and tolerances, captive/common fasteners, service access, drop-damage zones, corrosion/weather sealing, finish aging, replaceable lamps/body panels, end-of-life battery handling and material separation. Sustainability claims require evidence across the lifecycle.
7. **Verification:** package CAD and mass reconciliation, rider buck, interface/charging mockups, aero and thermal evaluation, structure/dynamics, then physical validation. These activities support each other; attractive renders cannot close engineering gates.

Manufacturer information reviewed October 25, 2026. Prices are listed starting prices in their respective markets, not equivalent delivered prices. Test cycles and range conditions differ; these are not a normalized performance ranking. Availability, transactions and buyer demand were not independently established.

|---|---|---|

| [Verge TS Pro](https://www.vergemotorcycles.com/ts-pro/) | Starts $29,900; 518 lb; 124 mph; manufacturer advertises 370-mile city and 196-mile highway estimates with regulatory figure TBC. | Spectacular architecture and ambitious range claims are already contested territory. Treat new battery/charge claims as manufacturer statements, not verified benchmark results. |
| [Maeving RM1S](https://maeving.com/en-us/products/maeving-rm1s-electric-motorbike?model=MA2132RUK) | Page presents $9,995 starting price, classic identity, removable batteries and 10 L storage. | A tightly defined use case can produce a coherent emotional product. Removable small packs are not transferable to Bavuso's heavy central pack. |

These products suggest a positioning opportunity around compact highway usefulness and ownership confidence. They do not prove an unserved market, a viable selling price, or a sales forecast. Recruit existing road riders, returning riders and EV owners separately; ask about actual trips, current alternatives, charging access, purchase barriers and willingness to pay before treating any group as the established customer.

Useful design precedents include the integrated road-sport cockpit/body relationship of the [Ducati SuperSport](https://www.ducati.com/us/en/bikes/supersport/supersport-950), architecture-led identity and storage in the [BMW CE 04](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/usa/article/detail/T0337286EN_US/the-new-bmw-ce-04), and recognition through lighting described in [BMW's motorcycle design history](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/news/general/2023/100-years-bmw-motorrad.html). Their lesson is coherence, not copying forms or importing incompatible battery layouts. [Zero's owner resources](https://zeromotorcycles.com/en-us/owners) also show how manuals, storage/charging guidance, firmware information and service support contribute to the product beyond its shape.

## Rider evidence and development implications

- **Ground reach:** [BMW's ergonomics guidance](https://www.bmwmotorcycles.com/en/engineering/detail/comfort-ergonomics/ergonomics.html) explains the importance of seat width alongside height. Study a narrow seat nose with useful support farther rearward, and test inseam, torso and arm proportions rather than height alone. Do not claim a percentile fit range without defining the population and dataset.
- **Posture versus drag:** [Kirca and McGordon's Warwick study, Table II](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/180779/1/WRAP-Effect-rider-position-energy-consumption-23.pdf) measured Energica CdA of 0.500 upright, 0.472 intermediate and 0.347 tucked. Its single-rider, zero-yaw conditions are not Bavuso predictions. A mild forward lean alone cannot be assigned a large efficiency gain.
- **Noise versus protection:** [Southampton helmet-noise research](https://generic.wordpress.soton.ac.uk/isvr-new/wp-content/uploads/sites/422/2022/07/inter96mc.pdf) found screen wake location affected helmet noise. Evaluate different seated heights, helmets and screen edges; taller does not automatically mean quieter.
- **Wind handling:** a [2026 six-component motorcycle wind-tunnel study](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/structcivil/72A/0/72A_315/_article/-char/en) reinforces evaluating forces and moments across wind angles. Keep gust/stability evaluation separate from drag reduction.
- **Comfort as a system:** the [Honda NT1100 feature description](https://hondanews.com/en-US/releases/release-cf005034e20eb27505fd8e6bbb0353ee-2026-honda-nt1100-dct-features-benefits) combines screen adjustment, deflectors, seat cushioning, heated grips and cruise. Borrow the integrated approach while making each item's mass and complexity explicit.

Build an adjustable rider buck. Evaluate normal highway posture, relaxed city posture and feet-down parking posture; test mounting, full steering, pushing/backing, stand operation and rearward view. Follow with gloved/wet interface and inlet mockups, sun/night legibility, rain spray, gear carrying, wet restart and basic-service tasks. Reverse assist, heated grips, screen adjustment and built-in storage are candidates to evaluate, not adopted requirements.

For intended-market road equipment, reserve physical space now and establish a current, jurisdiction-specific requirements matrix before freezing details. [NHTSA's manufacturer portal](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-manufacturers) provides entry points for test procedures and manufacturer responsibilities; this memo does not complete compliance analysis.

## Design territories to compare

All three retain the same initial battery/rider/drive package. They are alternative expressions of one product, not separate models or permission to change requirements.

| Territory | Brand/customer hypothesis | Physical expression | Opportunity | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Quiet Precision — recommended lead** | Experienced rider seeking an elegant everyday road bike that can travel | Integrated fairing, composed horizontal stance, softly controlled surfaces, compact tail, approachable seat, modest forward lean; restrained trim and clear physical controls | Strong alignment of comfort, efficiency, simplicity and broad visual appeal | Could feel anonymous or appliance-like; needs an ownable silhouette and front identity |
| **Modern Streamliner — strongest challenger** | Design-led rider attracted to a distinctive, enduring electric object | More continuous rounded body, carefully shaped shoulders/nose and taper, integrated protection; exposed wheels and real cooling paths retain motorcycle character | Makes efficient travel visible and accommodates battery volume deliberately | Apparent bulk, hot-air management, yaw moments, service access and retro caricature; smoothness does not prove low drag |
| **Purposeful Road Sport — comparison direction** | Road rider wanting sport credibility with highway comfort | Tauter fairing, stronger front emphasis, visible functional lower structure and restrained technical details; adjustable comfortable posture | Immediate motorcycle desirability and category recognition | Crowded visual territory; tiny screen/high pegs/short tail can sacrifice core product benefits |

Do not prioritize naked roadster, scrambler, cruiser or track-replica concepts in the first round. Their characteristic exposure, geometry, posture or hardware introduces conflicts with the current highway/aero/compactness brief. They could be reconsidered only through an explicit engineering and positioning trade.

## First drafting round

Produce three proportion studies on a shared scale and component layout. Each needs side/front/top views, an ordinary highway rider, battery/motor/electronics overlays, cooling routes, road equipment and a visible service route. Compare screen/shoulder width, knee width, seat/bar/peg relationships, taper, visual mass and provisional body/accessory mass. Label each untested aerodynamic idea as a hypothesis.

Judge concepts in two stages: first reject package, fit, cooling or clearance failures; then compare desirability, distinctiveness, comfort potential, aero hypotheses, manufacture/repair and brand consistency. Do not award speculative aerodynamic scores as if they were measured.

Owner decisions to narrow before detailed surfaces: target price band and production ambition; primary rider/use; passenger and luggage needs; preferred character among these territories; desired ordinary posture; minimum home-charging convenience; acceptable charging experience and weather conditions. These are not prerequisites for preliminary comparison sketches, but they are prerequisites for freezing a product definition.
