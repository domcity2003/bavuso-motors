# Bavuso Motors — designer's starting brief

October 25, 2026. Codex (GPT-6 family). Read alongside [engineering-handoff.md](engineering-handoff.md); every dimension below is provisional unless identified as supplier body data.

## Preserve the product

200 miles at the 70 mph range baseline; 125+ mph; real Tesla charging compatibility; preferably under 400 lb; comfortable, confident highway use with 77 mph as the comfort/stability design speed. Compact, minimal, broad appeal. Weight is the first possible slight concession, with no revised ceiling agreed.

## Reserve engineering space before freezing the shape

| Region | Initial reservation | Design implications |
|---|---|---|
| Battery region | 600 mm fore/aft × 260 mm wide × 560 mm tall study envelope | Central and protected; preserve knee fit, steering/bump clearance, pack removal and fault vent routes. Final package may be shaped rather than rectangular |
| Motor | EMRAX 208 body: diameter 208 × length 85 mm | Installation envelope is larger: shaft, support, sensor, cooling, connections and manufacturer-required circulation |
| Inverter | CM200 body: 330 × 188 × 97 mm | Add connections, hose bends, cable bends and mounting access; not automatically stackable against the motor |
| AC charger | Compact catalog body: 373 × 224 × 76.5 mm | Candidate only; preserve cooling and access; this unit's listed input does not include 120 V household service |
| Thermal equipment | Unresolved radiator/fan/duct volumes | Provide real intake and exhaust paths; fan cooling must work parked; body cannot hide a nonexistent radiator |
| Charging inlet | Native NACS; actual inlet drawing pending | Consider parked cable reach, handle clearance, inlet temperature and rain drainage; do not promise network access from the connector |
| Rider | Adjustable seat/bar/peg relationship | Use physical fit tests across riders; ordinary highway posture must achieve the aerodynamic target |

Supplier bodies are reference dimensions, not the clear space needed for installation. Avoid changing any of these into final manufacturing hard points yet.

## Targets to guide sketches

- **Total bike-plus-rider CdA target: at or below 0.33 m²** at the ordinary highway position. At 0.35, the reference model delivers only about 209 new-battery miles in still air; at 0.40, about 187.
- Explore 1,400–1,450 mm wheelbase, 780–820 mm seat height, 17-inch wheels and 120/70 front / 160/60 rear tires. These establish a study scale, not a validated geometry.
- Preserve approximately 120–140 mm wheel-travel exploration and full steering movement. Check loaded ride height, maximum bump, tire growth and lean clearance in CAD.
- Use adjustable rider contact points, helmet airflow/buffeting evaluation, cruise control and heat discharge away from the rider.
- At 77 mph, evaluate crosswinds and yaw/roll/steering moments. A streamlined side view does not establish gust stability.
- Do not depend on a permanent racing tuck for the range claim.

## Weight reality

Current allocated reference: **433 lb before contingency / 446 lb with allowance**. Several component masses are still targets, not measured parts. An aggressive **396 lb** path exists mathematically through 85% cell mass fraction and 88 kg outside the pack; with the same allowance method it becomes **407 lb**. Those numbers are development cases, not the owner's acceptance of a heavier bike.

The battery and body must be designed together. Better verified drag can reduce the battery requirement; attractive styling alone cannot earn that credit.

## What your design submission should contain

Side/front/top studies with dimensions; rider fit cases; component and service-space overlays; cooling and vent paths; wheel/steering/suspension clearances; and the shape assumptions to evaluate aerodynamically. Include an honest estimated body/accessory mass. Keep aesthetic choices open while these constraints converge.
